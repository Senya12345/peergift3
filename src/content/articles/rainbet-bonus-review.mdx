Rainbet gives new players two ways in and makes them choose. One is a 250% match up to
$2,100 plus 60 free spins on the first deposit. The other is a referral code that adds a
permanent 20% rakeback boost instead.

Most coverage leads with the match, because $2,100 is the bigger number. We think the
rakeback code is the better deal for nearly everyone, and the arithmetic behind that is
short enough to show in full.

## The match, with its conditions

These are the terms as we read them on 9 September 2026:

- Minimum qualifying deposit: $30.
- Wagering: 40x the deposit and the bonus combined.
- A 2% maximum-bet cap while the bonus is being cleared.
- Excluded from clearing: every Hacksaw Gaming and Nolimit City title, and any game with an
  RTP above 97.4%.

That last line gets less attention than it should. Shutting out the highest-RTP games
means the requirement has to be met on games where the house keeps more. And Hacksaw and
Nolimit City are two of the studios best known for the high-volatility slots people chase
big multipliers on — the reason many players want a bonus in the first place.

## Working it through

Take a $100 first deposit. The match adds $250, so $350 sits in the account. Forty times
that is **$14,000** in total bets before any of it can come out.

Now suppose you clear it on a slot returning 96%. Over $14,000 of bets, a 4% house edge
costs about **$560** on average. You started with $350.

That is the expected result, not a certain one. Variance is the whole appeal of slots, and
some players will clear the requirement and leave ahead. But the typical player runs out
of balance first, and that is the player the offer is priced for.

The 96% is our assumption, picked as an ordinary mid-range figure for mainstream slots. A
lower-RTP game makes the maths worse. The games that would make it better are the ones the
terms exclude.

## The rakeback route

The alternative returns a share of your play continuously, with no wagering requirement on
what it pays. Rainbet lets you claim it every ten minutes.

We could not find Rainbet's own definition of what the 20% is calculated on. At most crypto
casinos, rakeback is a percentage of the house edge on each bet — the casino's expected
profit — rather than of the stake. If that holds here, the same $14,000 of play at a 4%
edge would return around **$112**, withdrawable as it accrues.

$112 is less than $250. It is also money you can actually leave with, rather than a
balance you are statistically unlikely to unlock. Several independent reviews came to the
same conclusion for anyone playing real volume, and so did we.

## When the match still makes sense

One case. If you treat the deposit as spent — an entertainment budget, not something you
expect back — $350 of balance on a $100 deposit is simply more play, even if it most likely
ends at zero. Take the match, stay under the bet cap, and don't deposit expecting to
withdraw.

Anyone planning to come back regularly should use the code.
